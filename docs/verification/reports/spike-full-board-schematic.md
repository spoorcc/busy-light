## 1. What was built
One A3 sheet, drawn by `layout_full.py` in the same hand-laid style as the gate sheet (wires for local connections, net labels for the rest, power symbols, captions, title block). Sections:
- **USB-C input:** J1 (KiCad `USB_C_Receptacle_USB2.0_16P`), a TVS on VBUS, an ESD part on D+/D- (`USBLC6-2P6`), CC1 and CC2 only to the CC IC.
- **CC detection and the ADR-0016 gate:** U1 (TUSB321, generated box symbol, PORT tied low, VBUS_DET through 887 kohm, pull-ups to the IC supply), two 3.3 V inverters (OUT1 and OUT2), the two 3-input ANDs, pull-downs, the two ILIM MOSFET switches (same refs as the gate fragment).
- **Hold-off:** U5 (TPS3840DL40 candidate, generated box), delay capacitor, pull-up to the IC supply.
- **Current limit:** U7 TPS2553 (generated box), EN tied to VBUS, base ILIM resistor plus the two switched ones, FAULT_N pull-up, output capacitor.
- **Supply:** U8 3.3 V regulator (placeholder box, part not chosen), input and output capacitors, rail markers with power flags.
- **MCU:** U9 ESP32-C3-MINI-1 (generated box), EN pull-up and capacitor, USB, UART, boot, readback inputs.
- **Button (HW-007):** series resistor, pull-up, ESD diode, switch.
- **Buffers:** `HOLD_OK` (5 V) to 3.3 V buffer for the MCU; 5 V LED data buffer (HW-002) with pull-down.
- **LEDs (HW-001, HW-002):** 16 WS2812B in a chain (two rows of eight), 100 nF each, 330 ohm series resistor at the first LED, a bulk capacitor on 5 V. The sheet shows a chain, not the circle: the geometry is a PCB matter.
- **Test points (HW-006):** 5 V, 3.3 V, GND, LED data, EN, BOOT (UART points removed, see F1 update).
- **Size:** 84 part references (16 LEDs, 16 LED capacitors, 6 test points, ICs, boxes, resistors and capacitors), plus power symbols and flags (221 symbol instances in the file), 52 nets with more than one pin.

## 3. Findings

**Goldfish review of this sheet (2026-11-05, `review-full-board-goldfish.md`, nothing was run by the reviewer):**
- H1 GPIO2 and GPIO8 floated (module datasheet Table 4-1): **fixed**, R16 and R17 10 k to 3V3 (GPIO2 against glitches, GPIO8 = 1 for download boot and ROM printing left on; Table 4-3); test added.
- H2 tests could not catch level errors: **partly fixed** (connection-based test, negative check run); the intent table still comes from the drawing script, so it proves the drawing matches the intent only.
- M1 D+ pull-up back-feed into VBUS_IC through the USBLC6 (**partly examined**: USBLC6-2 datasheet read; pins 1/6 and 3/4 are tied pairs, GND 2, VBUS 5, as drawn; pin 5 is on VBUS_IC and the I/O pins have steering diodes to it, so any 3V3-side pull-up on USB_DP, such as the ESP32-C3 internal D+ pull-up, if enabled, can forward-bias into VBUS_IC when VBUS is gone; no external D+ pull-up is drawn; ESP32-C3 TRM v1.4 read (IO MUX GPIO table note, chapter 30 register USB_SERIAL_JTAG_CONF0_REG): the GPIO19 USB pull-up value is 1 by default, so the D+ pull-up resistor is enabled after reset (before any firmware runs); software can change it with USB_SERIAL_JTAG_PAD_PULL_OVERRIDE and DP_PULLUP; the value is selectable 2.2 kohm or 1.1 kohm (PULLUP_VALUE), reset default of that bit not read with certainty (the register figure text is garbled); the eFuse USB pin exchange does not move the pull-up (TRM note, design flaw); estimate: about 1.2 mA (2.2 kohm) to 2.5 mA (1.1 kohm) is the short-circuit current with a 0.6 V diode drop; open-circuit VBUS_IC is near 2.7 V, loaded (about 0.18 mA of VBUS_IC loads) it is about 2.0 to 2.6 V, which straddles the TPS2553 UVLO rising threshold (2.35 to 2.45 V) and is above its EN VIH, but the switch cannot stay on: with the switch closed the regulator and MCU load (>= 20 mA) collapses VBUS_IC, and in the loop +5V stays at least 0.7 V above VBUS_IC (diode drop, pull-up drop, regulator dropout) against the 95 to 190 mV reverse-voltage trip, so the TPS2553 reverse-voltage protection turns it off and asserts FAULT_N (N2, analysis only, see `analysis-n1-n2-readback-backfeed.md`); the TUSB321 is below its 4.5 V minimum and HOLD_OK stays low, so the gates stay closed, but the VBUS_IC net is no longer 0 V; options: a series diode or a switch in the D+ pull-up path, or the USB pins not used on the prototype; owner decision: author recommendation, accept for the prototype and measure on the bench VBUS_IC, +5V, 3V3 and FAULT_N (unplug decay, and 5 V on TP1 with the cable unplugged), recorded in the HW-010 proposal; recommendation holds after N2, rationale changed; not yet confirmed by the owner), M2 CC_OK_1A5 reads high with VBUS gone and 3V3 up (**examined, analysis only**: generator `hw/kicad/spike-gate/src/layout_full.py`: U1 OUT1 is open-drain with its pull-up R1 to VBUS_IC, U2 is an inverter on +3V3, CC_OK_1A5 is U2 pin 4 and goes to GPIO0 and to gate input 1; with VBUS gone VBUS_IC is about 0 V (or the M1 level), the TUSB321 is unpowered, OUT1_N sits low, so the inverter output CC_OK_1A5 is high; CC_OK_3A (U12, GPIO1) behaves the same; the gates then stay closed only through HOLD_OK, so a CC_OK reading is a false positive without VBUS; SW-009 (Draft) covers reading the hold-off and gate-enable state and shedding load, but does not say that the CC_OK inputs are invalid when VBUS is absent, and the CC-reading requirement (SW-007/SW-008 area, not re-read here) is not amended; author recommendation, not confirmed by the owner: add a firmware rule, drafted as a Proposed amendment of SW-009 (not an edit of the Approved or Draft text): "the firmware shall treat CC_OK_1A5 and CC_OK_3A as valid only while a VBUS-present indication is true and HOLD_OK_RB is high; otherwise it shall assume the lowest step"; open owner decision: the spike has no direct VBUS-present input to the MCU (all eight usable GPIOs are used, F1), so either HOLD_OK_RB high is accepted as the only validity condition (it is high only when VBUS_IC has been above the supervisor threshold and the hold-off has finished, so it implies VBUS), or a hardware change is made so that the inverter outputs are forced low without VBUS (for example a gate powered from VBUS_IC); recommended: HOLD_OK_RB as the validity condition, no hardware change; not run on the bench), M3 Zephyr console default on GPIO20/21 and ESP32-C3 ROM boot print (**examined, analysis only**: Zephyr tree in the local checkout `dfetch-example-zephyr/zephyr` (VERSION 4.3.99), board `esp32c3_devkitm`: `chosen` has `zephyr,console = &uart0` and `zephyr,shell-uart = &uart0`, defconfig sets `CONFIG_CONSOLE=y` and `CONFIG_UART_CONSOLE=y`, pinctrl `uart0_default` is `UART0_TX_GPIO21` with `output-high` and `UART0_RX_GPIO20` with `bias-pull-up`; so a Zephyr build for that board drives GPIO21 as a UART output and pulls GPIO20 up, which conflicts with the gate-enable readbacks; the project `sw/app/prj.conf` has no console setting yet and no board overlay was found, the project board definition is not read; ESP32-C3 TRM v1.4 section 7.3 Table 7.3-1 and EFUSE_UART_PRINT_CONTROL (eFuse register description): the ROM boot message goes by default to UART0 (GPIO21 TX) and to the USB Serial/JTAG controller; value 0 prints always and ignores GPIO8, value 1 prints when GPIO8 is low, value 2 prints when GPIO8 is high, value 3 never prints; GPIO8 is a strapping pin that only selects print behaviour, it is not an output; the generator pulls GPIO8 up (R17), so value 1 disables the print in this design, value 3 disables it regardless; the TRM states USB Serial/JTAG ROM printing is separate (EFUSE_USB_PRINT_CHANNEL); eFuses are one-time programmable, which was not checked against the TRM text beyond the register list; the second-stage bootloader output and its pins were not read; author recommendation, not confirmed by the owner: (1) in the application, set `CONFIG_CONSOLE=n`, `CONFIG_UART_CONSOLE=n`, `CONFIG_SERIAL=n` (or `status = "disabled"` for uart0 in a board overlay) and `CONFIG_LOG` to a backend that does not use UART0, so firmware never configures GPIO20/21 as UART; risk: no serial log on the prototype, debug only through USB Serial/JTAG (`CONFIG_USB_DEVICE_STACK` CDC is a separate software choice, not examined) or a log in RAM; (2) cover the ROM print, which no software setting can: either program EFUSE_UART_PRINT_CONTROL = 3 on production boards (permanent, ROM output lost for field diagnosis, one-time and not reversible; confirm in the TRM before use) or accept the ROM print on the prototype and protect the readback with a series resistor; (3) series resistor: R14 and R15 are drawn as 1 k (placeholder, see F1), but the value is not calculated: it must keep the readback logic levels with the load the gate-enable driver presents, and the ROM TX drive current is not read from a datasheet value for GPIO21, so the 1 k is unverified (owner decision with a bench check: ROM boot with the readback connected, measure the EN3 level and the GPIO21 current); not run on hardware), M4 hold-off threshold about 4.0 V below the TUSB321 4.5 V minimum (**examined, analysis only**: TPS3840 SNVSB03E and TUSB321 SLLSEO6C read, numbers in `datasheet-evidence-supervisor.md`; the DL40 hold-off timer starts when VBUS_IC rises through VIT+ = 4.115 to 4.285 V, while the TUSB321 is specified for VDD from 4.5 V (SLLSEO6C, recommended range 4.5 to 5.5 V); so a source that sits between about 4.3 V and 4.5 V for 200 ms or more would let the hold-off finish while the CC IC is out of its specified supply range, and the datasheet gives no output state for that case; the fix by threshold does not work: DL44 releases at 4.509 to 4.691 V, above the 4.5 V VBUS minimum the design assumes, so a legitimate source could keep the gate closed (fails safe, but loses the 1.5 A step), and DL42 has 12 mV margin; a source in the 4.3 to 4.5 V band is not a normal USB source (USB specification not read); author recommendation: keep DL40, accept, and add a bench check to the HW-010 acceptance criteria: ramp VBUS_IC slowly from 3.5 V to 5 V with a default-current source and show that no enable output rises; not yet confirmed by the owner; not run on the bench), M5 "ADR" pin and VCONN on the TUSB321 symbol: **M5 fixed** (datasheet SLLSEO6C pin table read: the symbol had an invented ADR pin and lacked CURRENT_MODE, VCONN_FAULT, ID and DIR; - **F1 update (2026-11-04, owner chose option b):** UART test points removed; GPIO20 and GPIO21 now read the enables of step 2 and step 3 through R14 and R15 (1 k, because GPIO21 is an output during ROM boot messages; changed to 100 k with R2/R3 = 10 k after review finding N1, see `analysis-n1-n2-readback-backfeed.md`); 6 test points; 50 nets, ERC 0 errors, intent equals drawing, 35 tests pass (with `GATE_NET=tests/data/gate.net`), two runs identical. Module pins 30 and 31 read in the datasheet. Original finding below.
- **F1 (HW-010 amendment): no GPIO is left for the gate-enable readback.** Used: GPIO0, 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 10 (the eight usable ones), 18 and 19 (USB), 20 and 21 (UART test points), 9 (boot test point), EN. The amendment asks for a readback of each gated step enable (EN2, EN3) as well as the hold-off. Options: (a) an I/O expander (one more part); (b) give GPIO20 and GPIO21 to the readbacks and drop the UART test points (HW-006 says programming and self-test go through the USB-C connector alone, so the UART points may be optional: owner decision); (c) read CC_OK and HOLD_OK only and drop the enable readbacks (the gate state is then inferred, not seen); (d) use the strapping pins GPIO2 or GPIO8 as inputs (risky, not examined). I recommend (b) or (c) for the first prototype.
- **F2: the CC IC outputs sit at 5 V.** The pull-ups go to `VBUS_IC` (the output pins are rated VDD + 0.3 V, `datasheet-evidence-supervisor.md`), so the firmware must read the 3.3 V inverter outputs (`CC_OK_1A5`, `CC_OK_3A`), never the raw pins. A test checks that no 5 V net is on an MCU pin. That an ESP32-C3 GPIO is not 5 V tolerant is from general knowledge and **not read** in the module datasheet.
- **F3: `HOLD_OK` (5 V) enters a 3.3 V buffer (U10, 74LVC1G125).** The 5 V input is within the rating of the 74LVC1G04 and 74LVC1G11 (read); the 74LVC1G125 datasheet (SCES223U) is now **read**: VI up to 5.5 V recommended with VCC 1.65 to 5.5 V, and Ioff covers VCC = 0 V with 5.5 V on the pin (+-10 uA max); pin order 1 OE, 2 A, 3 GND, 4 Y, 5 VCC matches the drawing; OE is active low and tied to GND; the Y behaviour for VCC between 0 V and 1.65 V is **not found**; details in `datasheet-evidence-switch-buffers.md`.
- **F4: the LED buffer (U11, 74AHCT1G125) runs on 5 V with a 3.3 V input.** HW-002 asks for a TTL-compatible input; the AHCT datasheet (SCLS378P) is now **read**: VCC 4.5 to 5.5 V recommended, VIH min 2 V and VIL max 0.8 V in that range, none given for 3.3 V supply (not used so here); pin order matches; no partial-power-down (Ioff) feature stated; details in `datasheet-evidence-switch-buffers.md`; ESP32-C3 output level not compared.
- **F5: second CC output.** OUT2 is used for a second reading (`CC_OK_3A`, an extra inverter U12 and pull-up R7); the TUSB321 table says OUT2 is not a clean 3 A signal (default 900 mA case), so its meaning in the firmware is open (`cc-ic-datasheet-evidence.md` row 5).
- **F6: sequence of rails.** The CC IC, the supervisor and the TPS2553 input are on raw VBUS; the LEDs and the regulator are on the switched `+5V`. The 3.3 V rail therefore drops when VBUS drops, which makes the "VBUS gone, 3.3 V still up" case of the goldfish review (H1) a short decay, not a steady state. It is still not simulated.
- **F7: TPS2553 pin order.** IN 1, GND 2, EN 3, FAULT 4, ILIM 5, OUT 6 is now **read** in SLVS841G Table 5-1 (DBV SOT-23-6, TPS2553 active-high EN); it matches the drawing; the ILIM resistor values are still `tbd`, so the 15 to 232 kohm range is not yet checked; details in `datasheet-evidence-switch-buffers.md`.
- **F8: part pins that are not read.** U9: the box still uses function names, so footprint mapping is untested; the module datasheet Table 3-1 was read later (GPIO18 pin 26 D-, GPIO19 pin 27 D+, GPIO20 pin 30, GPIO21 pin 31, see F1 and the HW-010 amendment) but the pin numbers are not drawn; U1: pins 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 are numbered (SLLSEO6C), VDD 12, GND 10, PORT 4, VBUS_DET 5, CC1 1, CC2 2 still use names; U7 pins are now read (F7).
- **F9: effort.** Placing and wiring about 80 references by hand-coded coordinates took many render-and-fix rounds; the writer is a drawing tool, not a placer. A real design needs either this effort per sheet or the KiCad GUI.
