## 1. Purpose
RV-008 H5 asked for the circuit behind "an open pull-up reads default" to be drawn. This spike builds the fragment of ADR-0016 option B from code, checks it with KiCad and tests its truth table. It does not choose parts.

## 2. What the fragment contains
Generic parts only: a placeholder 5-pin header for the CC detection IC (VDD, GND, open-drain OUT1_N low = at least 1.5 A, OUT2_N, spare); R1 100 kohm pull-up on OUT1_N; U2 74LVC1G04 inverter giving CC_OK_1A5; two 74LVC1G08 AND gates U3 and U4 (CC_OK AND select GPIO for step 2 and step 3); 100 kohm pull-downs R2 to R5 on both select inputs and both enable outputs; two 2N7002 with placeholder ILIM resistors. Every part is a stock KiCad symbol; no part number of ADR-0016 item 3 is fixed.

## 4. Findings
- **F1.** The fragment is feasible with two stock logic parts and passives; the cost is one more inverter and one AND per gated step (parts count for HW-010 and SYS-021). An IC with a high-active output, or a P-channel stage, would remove U2; not evaluated.
- **F2.** The truth table of ADR-0016 holds on the model for the single faults it lists. The model shares the one IC path between both steps, which is the H1 weakness of RV-008, now visible in the netlist (`test_both_ands_share_the_same_ic_path`).
- **F3.** SKiDL's schematic writer dropped a pin of the second unit of a dual gate symbol. The spike uses single gates. ADR-0012 already prefers an own deterministic writer; this supports that preference but does not prove it is needed for a bigger design.
- **F4.** The SKiDL router is not deterministic and failed or dropped pins in several tries. ERC alone is not enough: the parity check found a missing connection that ERC did not report. Both checks are needed on any generated schematic.
- **F5.** Open: the 3.3 V logic level versus the IC output (open-drain pulled to 3.3 V is fine on paper, not checked against a datasheet), the AND gate input threshold, the inverter delay at plug-in (scenario C shows that an IC output glitch while the select line is high enables the step for as long as the glitch lasts; the pull-downs do not help, and the firmware must hold the select lines low until the IC outputs have been stable ([5] reads, SW-005), which SW-005 now requires), and the state of the select GPIO during reset (ADR-0010 E24, not found).
- **F6.** The two enable nets share one ILIM node; the spike does not model the TPS2553 ILIM network or the resistor values.

**Correction (datasheet read after this spike):** the schematic powers the placeholder IC from 3.3 V. The TUSB321 datasheet specifies VDD 4.5 to 5.5 V, so the IC is powered from VBUS and only the output pull-up goes to 3.3 V; its output pin rating is VDD + 0.3 V, so the power order matters. The datasheet does not state the OUT1 level before the first 133 ms debounce, so the glitch in scenario C remains an assumption. See `docs/verification/reports/cc-ic-datasheet-evidence.md` on branch `docs/review-actions`.

**Finding F7 (hold-off).** A plain CMOS input as timer threshold is too loose: the hold-off would span about 0.2 to 1.1 s, and the upper value breaks SYS-020 (limit set within [1] s of connection) if it runs after the firmware wait. Use a Schmitt-trigger input or a comparator with a reference for the timer. With a maximum hold-off of about 600 ms the sequence stays inside 1 s only because the hold-off (from the IC supply) and the firmware window of SW-005 (from the first valid read, after the 133 ms debounce) overlap: about 133 ms + 500 ms = 633 ms. HW-010 now states a maximum of [600] ms.

## Version 3 (2026-11-04, after HW-003 and HW-010 were Approved and after a goldfish review of version 2)
Changes: the IC is supplied from `VBUS_IC`; the hold-off is a **supervisor with a programmable delay** (placeholder part U5, delay capacitor C1, open-drain output pulled up to `VBUS_IC` by R6) instead of an RC with a diode; each gated step uses a 3-input AND (`74LVC1G11`, 6-pin footprint); the `ILIM` net is one net.
- **Why the supervisor:** the goldfish review found that the diode of version 2 discharged C1 only down to VBUS plus the diode drop, so a dip to about 3.5 V ended the hold-off while VBUS recovered; HW-010 requires a restart. The supervisor holds reset while VBUS is below its threshold and for the delay after. The pull-up goes to `VBUS_IC`, so an unpowered supervisor reads "not done". New single faults: output stuck high, delay capacitor shorted or open (hold-off lost); to be added to RISK-042 or a new risk by the owner.
- **Run, simulation (`sim/run_powerup.sh`, ngspice 47, behavioural, assumed thresholds):** scenarios A to I as expected. H (VBUS dip to 3.5 V after the hold-off was done, then an IC glitch while the IC debounces again) keeps the enable low; I (same without hold-off) pulses, so H can fail. The script now **fails** when ngspice gives no number (the goldfish review showed the old script passed on a broken netlist); that was checked by feeding a broken netlist. The earlier scenarios D to G of version 1 and 2 were run with the unfixed script and are superseded by this run.
- **Run, netlist and writer:** 18 tests pass (connectivity, no single-pin named nets, one ILIM net, 6-pin footprint on U3 and U4, supervisor wiring, truth table, writer determinism and label coverage). The own writer (`src/write_sch.py`) produced the schematic: ERC errors 0, netlist parity 0, two generations byte-identical. SKiDL's own schematic writer was not used (it did not finish on version 2).
- **Datasheets read:** SN74LVC1G11 pinout matches the KiCad symbol (A=1, GND=2, B=3, Y=4, VCC=5, C=6); SN74LVC1G17 matches too; a 5 V output into the 3.3 V AND input is within its input rating (5.5 V recommended, 6.5 V absolute). See `datasheet-evidence-gate-logic.md`. The supervisor datasheet is **not** read: threshold, delay accuracy and leakage of the delay pin are unknown.
- **F10 corrected.** The earlier statement that "a 20 % capacitor does not fit" was wrong (I had mixed two tolerance sets). `sim/holdoff_tolerance.py`, now with the datasheet thresholds, gives for the superseded RC: R 1 % and C 10 % 200 to 356 ms; C 20 % 200 to 437 ms (both within 200 to 600 ms). Leakage (up to 5 uA specified as a maximum across 1 Mohm) and capacitor DC-bias loss are not in these numbers, which is why the RC was dropped.
- **Version 3a (supervisor and pull-up rail, after `datasheet-evidence-supervisor.md` and `datasheet-evidence-inverter.md`):** U5 is now the candidate TPS3840DL40 with a 620 nF C0G capacitor (5 %) and R6 150 kohm (datasheet minimum 134 kohm). The CC IC output pull-up R1 goes to `VBUS_IC`, because the TUSB321 output pins are rated VDD + 0.3 V at most (Rev C, p. 4) and a 3.3 V pull-up outlives VBUS on unplug. The 74LVC1G04 takes 5.5 V inputs at any VCC and has Ioff of at most 10 uA at VCC = 0 (SCES214AF); its output level at VCC = 0 is **not stated** (outputs disabled), so the enable nets keep their pull-downs R2 and R3 and the AND gates are unpowered at the same time. Simulation (ngspice, behavioural, TPS3840 datasheet delay constants): J (minimum constant, capacitor 5 % low) first enable rise at 211 ms; K (maximum constant, capacitor 5 % high) 571 ms; both inside 200 to 600 ms. Netlist tests 18 pass; writer ERC 0, parity 0. Dip restart still rests on an assumed fast discharge of the delay capacitor (datasheets do not state the time): scenario H is a model, not evidence; TC-210 and RISK-044 carry the bench check.
- **Version 3b (after the second goldfish review, `review-gate-v3a-goldfish.md`):** U5 now has the TPS3840 DBV pin order (RESET 1, VDD 2, GND 3, MR 4, CT 5; SOT-23-5); MR is left open and whether it needs a tie-off is **not read**. The committed writer fixture `tests/data/gate.net` was the version 2 netlist, so the earlier "18 tests pass" and writer results were true only for fresh netlists in the scratch folder; the fixture is now the version 3b netlist and 20 tests pass from the commit. The truth-table model now has VBUS present/absent, the hold-off input and a supervisor stuck high: with VBUS gone and 3V3 up, "CC OK" is high and the hold-off is the only barrier (finding H1); a stuck-high supervisor then opens the gate. Scenarios L (VBUS to 0 V, then an IC glitch: closed) and M (dip only to 4.3 V: no restart, the glitch passes) are added; M documents a gap and is expected to pulse. Scenarios J and K restate the datasheet formula through the capacitor value; they show the arithmetic of the model, not a measurement. Whether the device can have 3V3 up while VBUS is gone (USB-only supply) is an assumption to confirm in the power architecture. SW-009 (Draft) asks for a gate-state readback line; adding it to HW-010 is an owner decision.
- **Not run on version 3:** thermal, the power order of the 5 V IC against the 3.3 V pull-up (the goldfish noted the 3.3 V pull-up on the IC output may exceed VDD + 0.3 V when VBUS goes away; open), the AND inputs when the 3.3 V rail is absent and `HOLD_OK` is 5 V (not checked), the PCB, a goldfish review of version 3, the GUI open of the generated schematic.

## 5. Not done
No PCB, no footprints checked, no 3D, no DRC, no ngspice, no BOM, no JLCPCB check, no review by a goldfish. The schematic is not a deliverable.

## 6. Recommendation
Keep the fragment as the reference for the truth table; draw the real schematic in the KiCad GUI or with the writer of ADR-0012 after HW-003 and HW-010 are Approved and a part is chosen. Add the parity check to any generated schematic. Replace the assumed glitch by the IC datasheet power-up behaviour before HW-010 is Approved; if the IC can glitch low at start-up, add an RC filter or a hold-off on CC_OK, and a test for it in TC-210.
